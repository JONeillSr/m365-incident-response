# Incident notification template

Rewrite the text below in your own voice before you use it. Keep the two `##` headings exactly as they are and keep one `**Subject:**` line under each: the mailer finds the sections by those headings. Replace every `<...>` placeholder. Leave `[Name]` in the second version; the mailer fills it in.

## Standard recipient version

**Subject:** Important: please disregard the <date> "<campaign subject>" email

I'm writing to apologize and to give you clear guidance about an email sent from my Microsoft 365 account on <date> with the subject **"<campaign subject>."** It appeared to come from <compromised address>.

My account had been compromised, and the message was sent through authenticated access to my mailbox. It was not an authorized message from me or a legitimate business request.

I've contained the incident: I changed the account credentials, revoked active sessions, disabled unauthorized devices, removed malicious mailbox rules, and reviewed forwarding, delegated access, application permissions, and related activity. I found no evidence of continued unauthorized activity after containment.

Please take the following action:

- Delete the message and do not reply to it.
- Do not use any link, attachment, payment instruction, or contact information in it.
- If you only received or deleted the message, receipt alone does not mean your account or device was compromised.
- If you opened an attachment, followed a link, entered credentials, approved a sign-in request, sent information, or acted on a payment request, contact your IT or security team promptly. Review your account's sign-ins and active sessions, and change any credentials you entered.

If you or your security team believe the message caused a problem, I'll help at no cost with triage directly related to this incident. I will never ask you for a password, MFA code, or other authentication secret.

To verify this notice, contact me by phone or reply directly to this email rather than using anything from the fraudulent message.

Sincerely,

<your name>

## Personal follow-up for executives, security firms, and training partners

**Subject:** Personal follow-up regarding the fraudulent <date> email

Hi [Name],

I wanted you to hear this from me directly because I value our working relationship.

On <date>, a fraudulent email titled **"<campaign subject>"** was sent through authenticated access to my Microsoft 365 mailbox. I didn't send or authorize it. I've contained the account, revoked the attacker's access, disabled unauthorized devices, removed malicious rules, and completed an identity, mailbox, and message-trace investigation.

I'm sorry that an address associated with me was used this way, and I'm not asking you to treat it as less serious because I was also a victim of it.

If anyone at your organization interacted with the message, I'll help at no cost and am happy to work directly with your IT team. I can also walk you through what I found and the controls I've put in place since.

<your name>
